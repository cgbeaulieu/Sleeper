The First Down Rule
Is A Quarterback Tax

We have been calling the first-down bonus a hidden edge for receivers. A full season of data says it pays every skill position about the same, and charges quarterbacks for the privilege.

A correction

We said this was a receiver edge

The draft coverage made a claim that has been sitting in our notes as a live thread ever since. Our scoring pays half a point for rushing and receiving first downs and nothing for passing ones, and we said that this rewards receivers in a way nobody in the league had drafted for.

Having now scored a full season through the rule, that is not what it does. The first-down bonus pays running backs, receivers and tight ends at close to the same rate. What it actually does is charge quarterbacks.

2025, full season

Every position gets about a tenth of its points from first downs, except one

Taking the top twelve players at each position from last season and scoring them through our settings, then rescoring them with the first-down bonus removed, the share of each position's points that comes from first downs looks like this.

Running backs do best of the four and receivers are close behind them, but the difference between 11.0% and 10.4% is not a market inefficiency anyone can draft around. The gap that matters is the one at the bottom. A top-12 quarterback earns 11.4 points a season from first downs and a top-12 running back earns 34.9, because the only first downs a quarterback is paid for are the ones he runs for himself.

The mechanism is in the rule itself. On a ten-yard completion that moves the chains, the receiver is paid 0.5 for the catch, 1.0 for the yards and 0.5 for the first down, so he collects 2.00. The quarterback who threw it collects 0.40. He is paid for the yards and nothing for the conversion.

What it costs

It cuts the quarterback premium by more than half

Quarterbacks still score more than anyone else here. The top twelve of them put up 4020.0 points last season against 3796.8 for the top twelve running backs, so the position is ahead by 223.2 points across a dozen players. Remove the first-down bonus from both groups and that lead grows to 505.7.

The rule takes 282.5 points of quarterback advantage off the board, which is a little over half of it. In a superflex league where two quarterbacks start, that is the difference between a position you have to pay for early and a position you can wait on.

Last season's top three finishers took their first quarterback at picks 54, 77 and 82. This is a reason why that worked.

We wrote in the draft coverage that waiting on quarterback had worked in 2025 and left it as a pattern without an explanation. The scoring format is at least part of the explanation. It is one season of evidence and ten teams, so it is not a law, but the mechanism is real and it is in the settings rather than in the results.

Week 1, 2026

The early returns say the same thing

The biggest individual beneficiary of the rule in week 1 was Jahmyr Gibbs, who converted enough first downs to collect 6.50 points from the bonus alone, 17% of his 37.60. Kenneth Walker and Bijan Robinson each took 4.00, and Ashton Jeanty 3.50. The best any quarterback managed was Drake Maye at 2.50.

Chris Olave led all receivers at 3.50, which is a good week and still below what four running backs did. One week is not evidence of much on its own, and it points the same direction the season does.

Three teams in this league are carrying four quarterbacks each. KnightRider_85 and CaptainClark drafted that way, and OneManEra got there on Tuesday by adding Kirk Cousins and Tua Tagovailoa after losing twice.
